## What Students Should Know About Their Digital Footprint Before Job Hunting

Recruiters look candidates up before interviews. That is not a rumor from career services — hiring surveys have reported it for years, and anyone who has sat on a hiring panel has watched it happen. What shows up when someone searches your name is now part of your CV, whether you curated it or not.

The good news: auditing that footprint takes an afternoon, and most of the fixes are settings changes, not deletions. Here is the practical version.

### Step 1: Search yourself the way a recruiter would

Open a private browser window and search your full name, your name plus your university, and your name plus your city. Note the first two pages. Old forum posts, public photo albums, and tagged pictures surface here. For anything you control, tighten visibility or remove it. For anything you don't control but is inaccurate, use the platform's report or removal flow — most large platforms have one, and Google runs a personal-information removal request process for cases like exposed phone numbers and addresses.

### Step 2: Audit app permissions on your phone

This is where students leak the most data without noticing. Open your phone's permission manager and sort by the sensitive ones: location, microphone, contacts, and photo library. A flashlight app with location access, a wallpaper app reading your contacts — revoke anything an app does not need for its core job. On Android, prefer "ask every time" or "only while using the app" over blanket grants, and remove accounts and apps you no longer use instead of leaving them dormant with full access.

### Step 3: Separate your identities

You do not need to delete social media. You need compartments: one professional presence (LinkedIn, a portfolio or GitHub, anything you would link on a CV) that is public and polished, and one private life with locked-down visibility. Use different profile photos for each so image search does not bridge them. Keep the email address on your CV separate from the one tied to old accounts.

### Step 4: Reduce what you broadcast going forward

Every new account, quiz login, and "sign in with Google" extends the footprint. Before signing up for anything, ask whether it needs an account at all. When it does, give it the minimum: no real birthdate where a fake year works, no phone number where email suffices, location off unless the feature genuinely needs it. Free services funded by advertising treat your behavioral data as inventory — that is the trade, and you should make it consciously.

Students who want the full version of this audit — including how to check what Google itself has stored about you and how to replace data-hungry default apps with private alternatives — can walk through it in our [DeGoogle Starter Pack: 2026 Migration and Telemetry Audit](https://www.praveentechworld.com/blog/degoogle-starter-pack-complete-guide), which documents each step with screenshots from real test devices.

### The habit that matters

Do this audit once a year, the same week you update your CV. Footprints grow quietly; an annual review keeps yours intentional. Future you, sitting across from a hiring manager who already Googled you, will be glad past you spent the afternoon.
