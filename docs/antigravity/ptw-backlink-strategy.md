# PraveenTechWorld backlink and authority strategy

## Decision

Start backlink work now, but keep it selective and evidence-led. Excellent content can earn links, but a new site should not assume discovery will happen automatically. The operating model is **create something worth citing, put it in front of the right people, and never manufacture links**.

## Why now

The GSC export shows a large authority gap: many pages receive impressions in positions 30–90, while a smaller set reaches positions 7–15. Internal links will help distribute relevance, but external references are still useful for trust, discovery, and referral traffic.

## Linkable assets to build

1. **Windows diagnostic decision trees** — Event ID 13 vs Event ID 153, RAM vs driver vs PSU, and the KB5121003 evidence checklist.
2. **Google indexing setup checklist** — a source-backed guide that clearly separates crawling, indexing, ranking, URL Inspection, sitemaps, and IndexNow.
3. **Original, reproducible data** — small benchmark or troubleshooting datasets with dates, hardware/software versions, methodology, and downloadable raw data.
4. **Useful public tools** — safe PowerShell checks, sitemap validators, or log-triage snippets in a public GitHub repository with documentation pages on the site.
5. **Comparison tables that stay current** — password-manager pricing/features only when each row has a dated primary source.

## Ethical acquisition channels

- **Community contribution:** answer relevant Reddit, Stack Overflow, Microsoft Q&A, DEV, and forum questions with the fix in the post itself. Link only when the full diagnostic context is genuinely useful. Never automate posting or drop the same URL repeatedly.
- **Resource-page outreach:** contact maintainers of Windows, SEO, sysadmin, and local-AI resource pages with one specific asset that fills a documented gap.
- **Expert/source outreach:** when an article uses a vendor’s documented workaround or a researcher’s dataset, send the finished citation to that source. Ask for correction or inclusion only when appropriate; do not demand a link.
- **Original data outreach:** publish a small study or dataset, then pitch the finding to technical newsletters, journalists, and specialist communities.
- **Selective guest contributions:** write genuinely useful tutorials for relevant publications with a natural author/site reference. Avoid thin “guest post” networks.

## 30-day operating cadence

- Week 1: prepare three linkable assets and a short evidence page for each.
- Week 2: identify 20 relevant pages or discussions; record why each would benefit from the asset.
- Week 3: make 5–10 highly personalized contacts and contribute to 3–5 discussions without link-first behavior.
- Week 4: review referring domains, referral sessions, target-page impressions, and position changes. Keep only channels that produce qualified discovery.

## Guardrails

- No purchased bulk links, PBNs, automated comments, spun guest posts, exact-match anchor campaigns, or fake “editorial” placements.
- Prefer branded anchors, article titles, and plain URLs over repeated exact-match keywords.
- Do not ask communities to upvote or endorse content they have not read.
- A link is a bonus; the primary goal is a useful answer and a real referral visitor.

## Measurement

Track new referring domains, linking-page relevance, referral sessions, assisted conversions when monetization begins, target-page impressions, CTR, and average position. Raw backlink count is not a growth KPI.

## Bottom line

Great content can earn passive links, but waiting passively is slow for a new site. Use internal links immediately, create citation-worthy assets, and perform small, human outreach batches. This compounds authority without putting the domain at risk.
