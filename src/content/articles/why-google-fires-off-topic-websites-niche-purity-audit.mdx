## How Google Restricts Unfocused Websites

When an established domain publishes content outside its proven core, Google responds with three distinct mechanisms.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 HOW GOOGLE PUNISHES OFF-TOPIC PUBLISHING                │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  1. MANUAL ACTIONS (Site Reputation Abuse)
     - Target: Large publishers renting subdomains to affiliate brokers.
     - Impact: Complete deindexing of off-topic directories overnight.
     - Examples: Forbes Advisor coupon wipes, CNN Underscored manual penalties.

  2. ALGORITHMIC CONFINEMENT (siteFocusScore & siteRadius)
     - Target: Mid-sized blogs expanding into broad, unearned verticals.
     - Impact: Content remains indexed but cannot break past page 4.
     - Mechanics: Leaked API signals restrict topical domain authority.

  3. SILENT STARVATION (AI Overviews & Zero-Click Bleed)
     - Target: Generic summary content without first-hand benchmarks.
     - Impact: Position 1 CTR drops by 58 percent; 68 percent zero clicks.
     - Defense: Get cited directly in AI Overviews (+35 percent organic lift).
```

### 1. Manual Action for Site Reputation Abuse

In May 2024, Google enforced its new site reputation policy. It targeted legacy media brands that hosted third-party coupon sections on subdomains.

The fallout was fast. Forbes Advisor lost an estimated 24 million search visits almost overnight. Time Stamped saw search traffic drop by 97 percent. Google stated that unreviewed third-party posts break search rules.

### 2. Algorithmic Confinement to Core Topics

Many sites never receive a manual penalty in Google Search Console. Instead, they hit strict topic bounds.

The site still ranks for its initial core focus. However, new articles outside that boundary get suppressed. Google crawls the pages and indexes them. Then it strands them on page 5 or 6.

### 3. Silent Starvation via AI Overviews

Even when pages keep their rank, search clicks dry up. Studies from Ahrefs and SparkToro in 2026 reveal big shifts in search clicks.
- Position 1 organic click rates drop from 7.3 percent to 1.6 percent when AI Overviews appear. That is a 58 percent fall.
- Zero-click searches reached 68 percent across global queries in early 2026.
- Branded search queries gained 18.7 percent in reach under AI Overviews. Non-branded queries lost 20.0 percent.

If a page merely summarizes existing web pages, AI Overviews take all the clicks. But when an article shares real bench test data cited by the AI model, organic visits rise by 35 percent.

## Real Search Data: Winners and Losers from 100 Case Studies

The search reset between September 2023 and 2026 created clear winners and losers. Our team studied 100 case studies. We found clear operating traits.

### Sites that Lost Search Traffic

Sites that suffered heavy 80 to 95 percent traffic losses shared common flaws.
- **Keyword-First Posts.** Articles were written only because tools showed search volume. The team had no real experience.
- **Rented Trust.** Sites used domain age to rank for product roundups in crowded spaces.
- **No Physical Assets.** Authors reviewed gear or software without original lab photos, logs, or tear-downs.

Examples include independent review sites like HouseFresh (down 91 percent) and Retro Dodo (down 85 percent). Both wrote long reviews. Yet both fell to generic templates and big media brands.

### Sites that Gained Search Traffic

In contrast, sites that held or grew search traffic built real operational moats.
- **NapLab.** Traffic grew from 6,200 to over 132,000 monthly visits. They run real mattress testbenches with standardized drop tests, heat guns, and sensor cameras.
- **ShaverCheck.** Built a lasting technical moat around electric shavers. They use microscope foil photography and direct blade wear measurements.
- **First-Person Team Workbenches.** Sites that document exact terminal errors, hardware voltages, and Wireshark traces hold ground.

## The Topical Jurisdiction Doctrine: Inside the Leaked API

In May 2024, over 2,500 pages of internal Google search documentation leaked online. The files proved what engineers suspected for years. Google does not rank pages on external links alone.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                INTERNAL GOOGLE RELEVANCE & TOPIC SIGNALS                │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

  signal: siteFocusScore
  ├── Measures topical purity across the entire domain corpus.
  └── High score: 90%+ content tightly grouped around core entities.

  signal: siteRadius
  ├── Measures how far content strays from the primary entity centroid.
  └── Wide radius: Lowers site-wide ranking trust for specialized queries.

  signal: subdocRelevance
  ├── Measures whether individual sections contain direct, first-party answers.
  └── Rewards concise 130-170 word direct answer blocks.
```

### What siteFocusScore Means for Webmasters

The leak revealed an internal signal named `siteFocusScore`. This signal scores how focused a domain stays.

If an engineering site known for Linux debugging publishes a guide on credit cards, its `siteRadius` grows too wide. Rather than helping the new post, the topic drift hurts Google's trust across the entire site.

Search architect Koray Tugberk Gubur calls this the "Source Context" rule. A search engine must know what a site is about before granting rank. If a site tries to cover everything, Google trusts it for nothing.

## Case Studies: The Mathematical Proof for Content Pruning

When a site drifts off topic, the fix is surprising. You do not fix topic drift by writing more articles. You fix it by deleting dead pages.

Several search agencies saw big traffic jumps after deleting old pages.
- **Seer Interactive:** Removed thousands of unread URLs across client sites. Organic visits rose by 23 percent within 10 weeks.
- **Inflow Case Study:** Pruned 50 percent of dead catalog pages on a store. Organic sales surged by 104 percent.
- **QuickBooks Support:** Merged duplicate help articles into single master guides. Search visits climbed by 44 percent.
- **r/TechSEO Community Data:** Teams saw an average 32 percent traffic lift after pruning dead, zero-impression pages.

When you delete or redirect off-topic content, you free up crawl budget. You also raise your `siteFocusScore`.

## Why Hands-On Lab Testing Protects Search Traffic

In a web full of AI summaries and content scrapers, real hardware testing is the only real defense.

To keep our team from falling into generic summary traps, we apply three strict rules.

1. **First-Person Workbench Testing:** We test every software and hardware fix on real lab PCs. We share real terminal outputs, driver crash logs, and voltage checks.
2. **Interactive Offline Tools:** We build custom web tools like our VRAM Calculator and Password Manager Cost Tool. These tools give fast answers without tracking visitors.
3. **Automated CI Slop-Gate Tests:** Before any article goes live, our CI engine checks the text with our Slop-Gate tool. If the writing sounds robotic or vague, the build fails right away.
