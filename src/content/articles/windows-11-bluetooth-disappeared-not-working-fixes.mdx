Windows Bluetooth gone? Start by checking whether Device Manager still sees the adapter. If it does, work through airplane mode, Get Help, Bluetooth Support Service, driver state, and the accessory. If it does not, check hardware and firmware before reinstalling Windows.

Microsoft’s [Bluetooth troubleshooting guidance](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/fix-bluetooth-problems-in-windows-723e092f-03fa-4e7b-b1a2-3a9a8b1e6f98) follows the same basic boundary: check the PC and adapter before blaming the accessory. Work through one change at a time and record the result so you know which change mattered.

Use the fixes below in order. The early checks are reversible. Driver removal comes later, after you know whether the adapter is visible in Device Manager.

## 1. Check whether the PC actually has Bluetooth

**First confirm that Windows can see a Bluetooth adapter; a missing toggle is not proof that the radio is broken.**

Some desktop PCs do not include a Bluetooth radio, and a USB adapter can be unplugged or disabled. Check the computer's specifications or look for a Bluetooth entry in Device Manager before installing anything.

Press **Windows + X**, choose **Device Manager**, and look for **Bluetooth**. If it is missing, expand **Network adapters** and **Other devices** as well. A device with a warning icon may be the adapter with a missing driver.

## 2. Turn off airplane mode and check the hardware switch

**Restore the radio at the hardware and quick-settings level before changing drivers.**

Press **Windows + A** and make sure **Airplane mode** is off. Some laptops also have a physical wireless key or a vendor utility that can disable Bluetooth along with Wi-Fi.

Then open **Settings > Bluetooth & devices**. If the toggle appears, turn Bluetooth off, wait ten seconds, and turn it on again. This quick reset is useful when the radio is present but the Settings page is stuck.

## 3. Run the Windows Bluetooth troubleshooter

**Use Get Help to test the supported Windows repair path before making manual changes.**

On Windows 11, open the **Get Help** app and search for the Bluetooth troubleshooter. Let it check the radio, services, and common configuration problems before making driver changes.

If Get Help reports that no Bluetooth device exists, go back to Device Manager. That result points to detection, driver, hardware, or firmware rather than a pairing problem.

## 4. Restart the Bluetooth Support Service

**A stopped Bluetooth Support Service can make pairing fail even when the adapter and driver are healthy.**

Press **Windows + R**, type `services.msc`, and press Enter. Find **Bluetooth Support Service**, right-click it, and choose **Restart**. Open its properties and make sure the startup type is not set to **Disabled**.

Restart the PC after changing the service. Do not change unrelated services while troubleshooting; it makes the next result harder to interpret.

## 5. Update the adapter from Device Manager

**Install the driver for the exact adapter or computer model, not a generic driver package.**

If Bluetooth appears in Device Manager:

1. Expand **Bluetooth**.
2. Right-click the adapter, which may include the word **Radio** in its name.
3. Choose **Update driver > Search automatically for drivers**.
4. Restart Windows when prompted.

Windows may say the current driver is already installed even when the PC manufacturer has a newer package. If that happens, visit the support page for the exact laptop or motherboard model and download its Windows 11 Bluetooth driver. Avoid generic driver-download sites.

If Bluetooth disappeared immediately after Windows Update, compare the timing with our [Windows 11 update-error troubleshooting guide](/blog/fix-windows-11-update-errors-2026-troubleshooting) before changing several drivers at once.

## 6. Roll back a driver that broke after an update

**Roll back only when the timing points to a recent driver or Windows update.**

If Bluetooth worked before a recent Windows or driver update, open the adapter's **Properties > Driver** tab. Use **Roll Back Driver** if the button is available.

Only roll back the Bluetooth adapter that changed. Do not remove the Wi-Fi driver at the same time, because many laptop wireless cards expose both radios through related packages.

## 7. Reinstall the adapter cleanly

**Uninstalling the adapter is a later step because it changes the driver state and can hide the original clue.**

Use this when the update did not help:

1. In Device Manager, right-click the Bluetooth adapter.
2. Choose **Uninstall device** and confirm.
3. Shut down the PC completely.
4. Start it again and let Windows detect the hardware.
5. If the adapter does not return, open Device Manager and choose **Action > Scan for hardware changes**.

Microsoft notes that Windows will often reinstall the driver after a restart. If it does not, install the driver downloaded from the PC manufacturer's support page.

## 8. Fix a device that is visible but will not pair

**If the toggle works, troubleshoot the keyboard, mouse, speaker, or headset separately from the PC radio.**

If the Bluetooth toggle works but a keyboard, mouse, speaker, or headset will not connect, the PC adapter may be fine. Check the accessory instead:

- Charge it or replace its batteries.
- Put it into pairing mode, not merely powered-on mode.
- Move it close to the PC during pairing.
- Remove the old entry under **Settings > Bluetooth & devices > Devices**, then choose **Add device** and pair it again.
- Keep it away from unshielded USB 3.0 devices while testing; they can interfere with some Bluetooth connections.

For headphones or speakers that connect without sound, open **Settings > System > Sound** and choose the Bluetooth device under **Output**. This is separate from the pairing state.

If the sound control itself is missing or stuck after pairing, use our [Windows 11 volume-control troubleshooting guide](/blog/windows-11-volume-control-not-working-8-proven-fixes-for-2026) to test the Windows Audio path separately.

## If Bluetooth keeps disappearing after sleep

**A sleep-only failure points toward power management or a driver resume problem rather than initial pairing.**

Open the adapter's **Properties > Power Management** tab in Device Manager. If Windows offers **Allow the computer to turn off this device to save power**, clear it temporarily and test sleep and resume again.

Also check **Bluetooth Support Service** after waking the PC. If restarting the service restores the toggle, the issue is more likely a driver or power-state problem than a dead radio.

## When the adapter is still missing

**If the adapter remains absent from Device Manager after a clean restart and the manufacturer driver, check firmware or hardware before reinstalling Windows.**

If Bluetooth does not appear in Device Manager after a shutdown, a hardware-switch check, and a manufacturer driver install, inspect the BIOS or UEFI wireless settings and test with a known-good USB Bluetooth adapter. A missing adapter can be a hardware fault, a disconnected internal card, or a firmware setting. Reinstalling Windows is not the next sensible step.

When Windows search or Settings also behave strangely after the same update, check our [Windows 11 search fixes](/blog/windows-11-search-not-working-12-proven-fixes-for-2026) before assuming the Bluetooth hardware has failed.

## Read the Device Manager result before choosing the next fix

**The location of the adapter entry tells you whether to focus on detection, the driver, or the accessory.**

If **Bluetooth** appears as a category and the adapter has no warning icon, Windows has detected the radio. A pairing failure at that point is more likely to involve the accessory, a stale saved profile, interference, or the wrong output device. Remove the old device entry and pair it again instead of uninstalling the adapter.

If Bluetooth is listed with a yellow warning icon, open **Properties** and record the **Device status** text and error code. The code matters because “driver cannot start” and “device was stopped” do not point to the same repair. Save a screenshot or copy the text into the project notes before changing anything. That gives you a comparison point if a driver reinstall makes the message less specific.

If the category is missing but **Other devices** contains an unknown device, Windows may see the hardware without the correct driver. Download the Bluetooth package from the laptop, motherboard, or adapter manufacturer using the exact model number. Avoid driver aggregators that bundle unrelated utilities; they make it difficult to know what changed.

If no Bluetooth-related entry appears anywhere after a full shutdown, check the laptop’s wireless key, BIOS or UEFI setting, and the physical USB connection. On a desktop, confirm that the motherboard actually includes Bluetooth or that the USB adapter is plugged in. A Windows reinstall cannot repair a radio that the firmware or hardware does not expose.

## Collect one clean comparison before reinstalling

**A short before-and-after record prevents a driver change from turning troubleshooting into guesswork.**

Write down four observations:

1. whether the Bluetooth toggle exists;
2. whether Device Manager lists the adapter;
3. whether a known accessory appears during pairing;
4. whether the accessory connects but sends audio to another output.

Then change one variable and repeat the same checks. For example, restart the service and test the same headset. If that fails, update only the Bluetooth driver and repeat the test. This sequence is slower than clicking through every suggested fix, but it tells you which boundary changed.

The same approach helps after an update. If Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, and Windows Search all changed after the same reboot, record the update date and device-manager status before rolling anything back. Our [Windows 11 update-error guide](/blog/fix-windows-11-update-errors-2026-troubleshooting) covers the broader update path, while this article stays focused on the radio and pairing stack.

## FAQ: Bluetooth questions we check most often

### Why did Bluetooth disappear from Windows 11?

The common causes are airplane mode, a disabled hardware switch, a stopped service, a driver problem, a firmware setting, or a radio that Windows cannot detect. Start with Device Manager so you know which of those branches applies.

### Should I download a Bluetooth driver from a third-party site?

No. Use the support page for the exact laptop, motherboard, or adapter model. Generic driver sites can install the wrong package and add software that is unrelated to the radio.

### Why does Bluetooth connect but have no sound?

Pairing and audio output are separate. Open **Settings > System > Sound**, choose the Bluetooth device under **Output**, and then test again. If the sound controls are also failing, follow the [Windows 11 volume-control troubleshooting guide](/blog/windows-11-volume-control-not-working-8-proven-fixes-for-2026).

### Is a missing Bluetooth toggle proof that the adapter is dead?

No. The toggle can disappear when Windows cannot load the driver or when a service, hardware key, power state, or firmware setting disables the radio. A hardware fault becomes more likely only after the adapter stays absent from Device Manager through those checks.

## Summary: the safe Bluetooth troubleshooting order

Check hardware and airplane mode first. Run Get Help, restart Bluetooth Support Service, and update the exact adapter driver. Only then uninstall and reinstall it. If pairing alone is failing, reset the accessory and select it as the Windows sound output.

If the problem is actually audio after Bluetooth connects, our [Windows 11 volume-control troubleshooting guide](/blog/windows-11-volume-control-not-working-8-proven-fixes-for-2026) covers the separate Windows Audio path.

The whole comparison takes about **2 minutes** when the adapter is visible: check the toggle, inspect Device Manager, restart the service, and retry the same accessory. If the adapter is still absent after that controlled sequence, stop changing software and move to firmware or hardware checks.

For the first pass, record each boundary as a **100% binary result**: adapter present or absent, service running or stopped, and accessory paired or not paired. That simple record is more useful than changing three settings at once.
