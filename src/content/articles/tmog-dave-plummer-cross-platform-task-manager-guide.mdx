## Who Dave Plummer is, in one paragraph

Dave W. Plummer wrote the original Windows Task Manager at home in the mid-1990s while working at Microsoft, brought it inside after Windows NT architect Dave Cutler approved it, and shipped it with Windows NT 4.0 in 1996 at roughly 80KB. Microsoft's own engineering writing later called a Task Manager interface component "Dave's frame class," a fossil of his authorship still embedded in the utility. He also built Windows ZIP folder support and ported Space Cadet Pinball to NT, left Microsoft in 2003, and runs the Dave's Garage channel. When he says he knows what a task manager should measure, there is nobody alive with a longer claim.

## What TMOG actually is (architecture)

One shared C++20 measuring core defines every metric, action, model, and serialization format. Three native front ends render it: Swift and AppKit on macOS, C++ with Win32 and Direct2D/DirectComposition on Windows, C++ with Qt 6 on Linux. There is no Electron, no web view, no embedded browser — Plummer's stated reason is game-like response times, and the 60Hz refresh target (adjustable down to 30/15Hz) only makes sense on native code.

Free views: Summary, Performance, Processes, App history, Startup apps, Users, Services, and System info, plus a self-monitoring toggle that includes TMOG's own footprint in the readings. Pro views ($39.95): Power & frequency, Flight Recorder, Connections, Installed Apps, Drivers, Disk Space, and Benchmarks. Flight Recorder is the Pro feature that matters: it records the seven telemetry panels into one interchangeable `.tmogtrace` file so you can scrub back through a slowdown after the fact — a postmortem for the fans-spinning-at-3AM problem.

Plummer's design brief, in his own words via tmog.org: Activity Monitor's clarity with Task Manager's depth. The default theme is neon-drenched cyberpunk and unapologetically loud; it is also fully themeable, which PC Gamer's hands-on rightly suggests doing within five minutes.

## The vibe-coding story, accurately told

It started as an argument over whether anyone could vibe-code Microsoft Word. Plummer doubted it, picked Task Manager as the smaller target he knows better than anyone, and wrote a 107-page specification — his phrase is "some caffeine-addled PM from the 90s" — before letting Claude Code loose. First working builds across the foundations arrived in about four and a half hours and ran on the first try, written partly from a hospital chair while his son recovered from surgery. Then came roughly six weeks of refinement with two collaborators: the generation was the easy part, the specification and the review were the work. His warning, reported by Engadget from his own announcement: tell Claude "write a task manager" with no spec and you get hot garbage. The Register got the mission statement version: he picked Task Manager to see whether AI is ready for primetime or degrades to slop.

## Is it safe? The full check

TMOG's published privacy policy is unusually specific and unusually good. Automatic network calls are HTTPS GETs with no request body: an update-manifest check and a license-link check. No version, metrics, process data, hardware inventory, IP-stored logs beyond masked 30-day operational logs. Pro licensing is a signed entitlement verified entirely on your machine — no periodic check-ins, offline use continues if their server dies, and Lemon Squeezy (merchant of record) is the only party that ever sees payment data. The app even tells feedback reporters not to paste commands, paths, or usernames.

The real risk window was distribution, not telemetry: 0.1.1-era binaries were unsigned on all three platforms. That window is now closed on Windows: on September 28, 2026 we downloaded `TMOG-Task-Manager-Setup.exe` (7.2MB) directly from tmog.org and verified its Authenticode signature as **Valid**, issued to **CN=David Plummer, O=David Plummer, Redmond, Washington** by SSL.com with a trusted timestamp. SHA-256 of the verified copy: `96AECAD1C3F7FF14B7E4B29E75FEA358680A987531EFA8516C834ED38B9E0B78`. tmog.org lists the macOS 1.0.0 build as signed and notarized.

## Install it per OS

- **macOS 14+ (Apple Silicon and Intel):** download from tmog.org, open the app, approve in System Settings if Gatekeeper asks. Signed and notarized at 1.0.0.
- **Windows 11 (x64 and ARM64):** installer or zip from tmog.org. The 1.0.0 installer carries a Valid code signature issued to David Plummer — verify it yourself with `Get-AuthenticodeSignature` before running anything, and never bypass SmartScreen for copies obtained anywhere except tmog.org.
- **Linux (X11 or Wayland):** .deb for Debian/Ubuntu, AppImage, or tarball, in x64 and ARM64. One user-reported caveat from the community: the .deb wanted GLIBC_2.38, so check your distro's glibc before choosing the package format.

Who should pay: sysadmins and performance troubleshooters who diagnose slowdowns after the fact — Flight Recorder is the only tool in this category that replays the incident instead of describing the present. Who should not: anyone who just wants a prettier Task Manager; the free edition covers that completely. One honest caveat, quoted from Pro's own terms: buying Pro is not a commitment by the developer to ongoing development, maintenance, or support. You are buying today's build, not a roadmap.

## Honest limitations (what the skeptics got right)

PC Gamer's hands-on found TMOG's own footprint higher than stock Task Manager (roughly 120MB vs 105MB, 3–4% CPU vs under 1%, improvable by calming the visuals), per-process memory readings that diverge from Task Manager's (11GB vs 8GB for the same Chrome tabs in their test), and thermal readings that returned implausible values on their rig. A Linux tester measured 2–3 second latency opening Startup/Users/Services pages. These are beta-era findings on a fast-moving target, and they are exactly what our lab retest will re-measure against 1.0.0 RTM — including whether TMOG and Task Manager agree on the same Chrome tabs on our rig. If the numbers moved, this article gets updated with the new table and a dated changelog entry, not a silent edit.
