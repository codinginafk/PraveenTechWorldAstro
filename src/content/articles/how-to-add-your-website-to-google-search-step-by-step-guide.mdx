> **Direct Answer (Add Website to Google):** To make your website searchable on Google: (1) open [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console), (2) add your domain and verify ownership via a DNS TXT record, (3) submit your XML sitemap URL (e.g. `sitemap-index.xml`) under Sitemaps, and (4) use the URL Inspection tool to test your homepage and click **"Request Indexing"**. Initial crawling and discovery typically begin within 24 to 72 hours.

Yes—you can add your website to Google Search Console by creating a property, verifying ownership, submitting its XML sitemap, and requesting a crawl for a few important URLs. According to Google’s [recrawl documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/ask-google-to-recrawl), crawling can take days or weeks, and a request does not guarantee inclusion.

This guide covers the exact setup for your Astro, Next.js, WordPress, or ordinary hosted site. It separates discovery from indexing: a sitemap helps Google find URLs, while page quality, canonical signals, accessibility, and internal links influence whether Google keeps and ranks them.

There is no reliable “force indexing in 24 hours” method for ordinary blog pages. The goal is a clean, inspectable setup that gives search engines the right signals and gives you evidence when a page is not indexed.

## The three steps to add a website to Google Search

1. **Verify Domain Ownership:** Add a `google-site-verification` DNS TXT record at your domain registrar (Cloudflare / Namecheap).
2. **Submit Sitemap Index:** Submit `https://yourdomain.com/sitemap-index.xml` inside Google Search Console.
3. **Submit and inspect:** Submit the sitemap, then use URL Inspection for a small number of priority pages and monitor the Page Indexing report.

## Step 1: Add Your Website to Google Search Console (GSC)

Google Search Console is Google's official command center for site owners.

```text
[ Your Registrar (Cloudflare/Namecheap) ] ──► Add DNS TXT Record
                                                     │
   [ Google Search Console ] ◄── Verified Ownership Handshake
               │
               ▼
   [ Submit sitemap-index.xml ] ──► Googlebot Priority Queue
```text

1. Navigate to [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console) and log in with your Google account.
2. Select **Domain** as your property type (Recommended).
   - *Why Domain Property?* A Domain property covers all protocols (`http://`, `https://`) and subdomains (`www`, `blog`, `m`) under a single unified dashboard.
3. Enter your bare domain (e.g., `praveentechworld.com`) and click **Continue**.

## Step 2: Verify Domain Ownership via DNS TXT Record

To access search analytics and submit URLs, you must prove you own the domain.

### 📋 DNS TXT Setup Across Popular Registrars

| Registrar | Record Type | Name / Host | Value / Target |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---|
| **Cloudflare** | TXT | `@` | `google-site-verification=xxxxxxxxx` |
| **Namecheap** | TXT | `@` | `google-site-verification=xxxxxxxxx` |
| **GoDaddy** | TXT | `@` | `google-site-verification=xxxxxxxxx` |
| **Porkbun** | TXT | `@` | `google-site-verification=xxxxxxxxx` |

1. Copy the verification string starting with `google-site-verification=` from GSC.
2. Open your DNS management dashboard in Cloudflare, Namecheap, or GoDaddy.
3. Add a new **TXT Record**, set Host to `@`, paste the verification string, and set TTL to Auto.
4. Return to GSC and click **Verify**. DNS changes may take longer than a few minutes depending on the registrar and resolver cache.

> 💡 **Pro Tip:** Unlike HTML file uploads, DNS TXT verification remains permanently valid even if you migrate hosting servers, rebuild your frontend, or switch framework stacks.

## Step 3: Generate and submit your XML sitemap

An XML sitemap lists the canonical URLs you want Google to discover. It is a discovery aid, not a guarantee that every URL will be crawled or indexed. Keep redirected, blocked, duplicate, and `noindex` URLs out of it.

1. Locate your site's XML sitemap URL (usually `/sitemap-index.xml` or `/sitemap.xml`).
2. In GSC, click **Sitemaps** under the *Indexing* section in the left sidebar.
3. Type `sitemap-index.xml` into the *Add a new sitemap* box and click **Submit**.

```xml
<!-- Standard XML Sitemap Structure Example -->
<?xml version="1.0" encoding="UTF-8"?>
<urlset xmlns="http://www.sitemaps.org/schemas/sitemap/0.9">
  <url>
    <loc>https://www.praveentechworld.com/blog/how-to-index-your-website</loc>
    <lastmod>2026-08-14</lastmod>
    <priority>1.0</priority>
  </url>
</urlset>
```xml

## Step 4: Notify Bing with IndexNow

IndexNow is useful for notifying Bing and participating search engines when a URL is added, updated, or deleted. It does not replace a sitemap, quality review, or Google’s own crawling systems.

### The IndexNow protocol
Create an IndexNow key in Bing Webmaster Tools, host the key file at the required location, and submit changed URLs through the documented endpoint. Treat the response as a received notification, not a ranking promise.

```json
// IndexNow API JSON Payload
{
  "host": "www.praveentechworld.com",
  "key": "your-indexnow-api-key",
  "keyLocation": "https://www.praveentechworld.com/your-indexnow-api-key.txt",
  "urlList": [
    "https://www.praveentechworld.com/blog/new-article-1",
    "https://www.praveentechworld.com/blog/new-article-2"
  ]
}
```

### 1. Bing Webmaster Tools setup
1. Log into **Bing Webmaster Tools**.
2. Add and verify the site, or use the available import option.
3. Submit the sitemap and check the IndexNow report after sending updates.

### 2. What IndexNow does not do
IndexNow can speed up notification for participating engines. It does not automatically index every page, remove quality checks, or guarantee visibility on Yahoo, DuckDuckGo, or Google.

## Step 5: Troubleshooting Common Indexing Errors

If Google Search Console flags URLs in your **Indexing** report, here is how our team resolves them:

| GSC Status Error | Cause | Instant Fix |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Discovered - currently not indexed** | Google knows the URL but has not indexed it | Check canonical, quality, status code, internal links, and competing duplicates |
| **Crawled - currently not indexed** | Google crawled the page but did not select it | Improve usefulness and intent match; inspect canonical and duplicate signals |
| **Sitemap Couldn't Fetch** | Fetch, access, or XML problem | Test the live URL, validate XML, then resubmit the sitemap |
| **Blocked by robots.txt** | A robots rule blocks crawling | Correct the rule only after confirming the URL should be crawlable |

## Domain property or URL-prefix property?

**Choose a Domain property when you control DNS and want one view of the whole domain.** It includes HTTP, HTTPS, subdomains, and other protocol variations. DNS verification is required, but it avoids the common mistake of verifying only `https://www.example.com` while important pages live on another host.

**Choose a URL-prefix property when you need to verify one exact path or cannot edit DNS.** Google supports HTML files, HTML meta tags, Google Analytics, Google Tag Manager, and DNS methods depending on the property type. A URL-prefix property is narrower, so record the exact protocol, hostname, and path before you start.

Do not add several properties and assume they merge automatically. `http://example.com`, `https://example.com`, and `https://www.example.com` can report different data. Pick the property that matches the canonical URLs in your sitemap, then add other variants only when you have a reporting reason.

## What to inspect before requesting a crawl

**Make sure the live page is technically eligible before asking Google to revisit it.** Open the final URL in a private browser window and check these items:

1. The server returns a successful response rather than a login page, soft 404, or redirect chain.
2. The page has a self-referencing canonical URL or a deliberate canonical pointing to the preferred version.
3. The page is not marked `noindex` and is not blocked by `robots.txt`.
4. The main answer is visible without requiring a form submission, client-only navigation, or a cookie consent action that hides the content.
5. The sitemap lists the canonical URL, not an old slug, tracking URL, or redirected version.
6. At least one relevant page on your site links to it with descriptive anchor text.

If any of those checks fail, fix the site first. Repeated URL Inspection requests do not repair a canonical, robots, response, or content problem. They only create more crawl requests for the same unresolved signal.

## How to use URL Inspection correctly

**Use URL Inspection for a few important URLs, not every URL in a sitemap.** Paste the exact canonical URL into Search Console and read the indexed result. If the result is not current, use **Request indexing** once after the page is ready. Google documents a quota for individual requests and says repeated submissions do not make a page crawl faster.

After submitting, record the date, URL, and status in a small spreadsheet or note. Check again after several days, then use the Page Indexing report to see whether Google selected the page. If the page remains excluded, read the reason instead of submitting it repeatedly.

For a large site, submit the sitemap and improve the internal link graph. A sitemap helps discovery; it does not replace useful content or make every URL eligible. Remove old URLs from the sitemap after a slug change and add a direct 301 redirect from the old URL to the final page.

## Why a verified website still may not appear

Verification proves that you control the property. It does not promise rankings. A verified page can remain absent from search when:

- the page is too similar to another URL and Google chooses a different canonical;
- the content does not satisfy the query better than existing results;
- the page is thin, unfinished, or mainly repeats a template;
- the URL returns a successful-looking page but has a soft 404 experience;
- internal links are weak or the page is only reachable from a tag or search page;
- the site recently moved, changed domains, or has conflicting HTTP and HTTPS versions;
- the page is new and Google has not yet completed its crawl and indexing evaluation.

The fix depends on the reason. Add useful detail when the page is shallow, consolidate duplicates when two URLs answer the same question, and repair canonical or server signals when the technical reports disagree. Do not add filler paragraphs just to reach a word count.

## How to measure whether the setup worked

**Measure discovery and performance separately.** First check whether Google can fetch and index the URL. Then wait for Search Console performance data to accumulate. A page can be indexed but receive no impressions because the query demand is low, the position is weak, or the title does not match the searcher’s wording.

For each important page, record:

- canonical URL;
- sitemap submission date;
- URL Inspection result;
- first indexed date, when available;
- impressions, clicks, CTR, and average position over a comparable date range.

Compare a seven-day or fourteen-day window against the previous window. A same-day change is too noisy to prove that a sitemap submission caused a ranking improvement. Keep the page stable long enough to learn which change helped.

## A practical final checklist

Before you finish, confirm that:

- the correct Google Search Console property is verified;
- the canonical HTTPS sitemap is submitted;
- the sitemap contains only public, canonical URLs;
- the homepage and at least one relevant article link to the new page;
- the page passes the URL Inspection live test;
- the page has a useful title, description, heading, and answer—not a promise of instant indexing;
- old versions of the URL redirect to the current one;
- you have recorded the inspection date and will review the result later.

## Summary & Further Reading

To add your site to Google Search, verify ownership, submit a clean sitemap, request a crawl for a few priority URLs, and monitor Search Console. Use IndexNow separately for Bing notifications. Neither system guarantees instant indexing.
